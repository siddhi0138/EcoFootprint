# Plastic Resin Identification Codes (1-7)

Resin identification codes (the numbers 1-7 often stamped inside a triangle on plastic products) identify the type of resin used to make an item. They do NOT indicate whether a product is actually recyclable in your local program — despite resembling recycling symbols, these codes frequently contribute to "wishcycling" (contaminating recycling bins with unrecyclable items). In 2018, only 8.5% of plastic waste was recycled in the U.S.

## Code 1: PET (Polyethylene Terephthalate)
Common uses: Polyester fibers (polar fleece), thermoformed sheet, strapping, soft drink bottles, tote bags, furniture, carpet, paneling.
Recyclability: Picked up through most curbside recycling programs.

## Code 2: HDPE (High-Density Polyethylene)
Common uses: Bottles, grocery bags, milk jugs, recycling bins, agricultural pipe, base cups, car stops, playground equipment, plastic lumber.
Recyclability: Accepted through most curbside programs, though some allow only containers with necks.

## Code 3: PVC (Polyvinyl Chloride)
Common uses: Pipe, window profile, siding, fencing, flooring, shower curtains, lawn chairs, non-food bottles, children's toys.
Recyclability: Limited recycling volumes due to long product lifespan.

## Code 4: LDPE (Low-Density Polyethylene)
Common uses: Plastic bags, six-pack rings, various containers, dispensing bottles, wash bottles, tubing, molded laboratory equipment.
Recyclability: Not often recycled through curbside programs and is a significant source of plastic pollution. Can often be returned to retail stores for recycling.

## Code 5: PP (Polypropylene)
Common uses: Auto parts, industrial fibers, food containers, dishware.
Recyclability: Picked up through most curbside recycling programs.

## Code 6: PS (Polystyrene)
Common uses: Desk accessories, cafeteria trays, plastic utensils, coffee cup lids, toys, video cassettes, clamshell containers, packaging peanuts, insulation board, expanded polystyrene (Styrofoam).
Recyclability: Often not recycled through curbside programs as it is too lightweight to be economical to recycle; usually incinerated instead.

## Code 7: OTHER (Other Polymers)
Common uses: Bottles, plastic lumber applications, headlight lenses, safety shields/glasses. Includes polycarbonate, acrylic, nylon, polylactic acid (bio-based plastic from corn starch or sugarcane), and multi-layer packaging combining different resins.
Recyclability: Not typically recycled — mostly specialty-produced in limited volumes.

## Summary
Codes 1, 2, and 5 are the resins most likely to be accepted by local recycling programs. Codes 3, 6, and 7 are rarely recyclable through standard collection. Code 4 falls somewhere in between, depending on location.
