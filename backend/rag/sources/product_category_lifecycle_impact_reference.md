# Product Category Lifecycle Impact Reference

## Electronics (smartphones, laptops, tablets)

Source: Ericsson smartphone LCA research (https://www.ericsson.com/en/reports-and-papers/research-papers/life-cycle-assessment-of-a-smartphone); multiple published smartphone LCA studies.

Manufacturing dominates a smartphone's footprint, not the use phase: production (integrated circuits, PCBs, and assembly) accounts for roughly 80% of total lifecycle emissions. Reported total lifecycle emissions for a smartphone range from about 40-60 kg CO2e across studies, with wide variance by device size and study assumptions. Raw Materials (mining rare-earth/precious metals) and Manufacturing should be weighted far higher than Transportation, Use Phase, or End of Life for this category. End-of-life CO2 contribution is comparatively small, but e-waste toxicity/recyclability is a distinct concern worth flagging even when its carbon share is minor.

## Textiles / Clothing (cotton-based)

Source: Comparative T-shirt LCA study (https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/18/8/4070); T-shirt carbon footprint breakdown (https://onelessofficial.com/en-us/blogs/blog/carbon-footprint-of-a-t-shirt).

Raw Materials (cotton cultivation) dominates water use: growing 1kg of cotton fiber requires roughly 8,000-10,000+ litres of water, and materials account for over 90% of a garment's total water footprint. For carbon, Materials plus Use Phase (washing/drying) together account for over 95% of a cotton T-shirt's lifecycle emissions, with Manufacturing, Transportation, and End of Life contributing under 5% combined. Reported total footprint for a basic cotton T-shirt ranges roughly 3-11 kg CO2e depending on study assumptions and garment weight - keep figures modest and note the variance rather than quoting a single false-precision number.

## Plastic Packaging (PET bottles and similar)

Source: PET bottle LCA meta-analysis (https://www.mdpi.com/2071-1050/16/2/535); NAPCOR PET Life Cycle Assessment Report (https://napcor.com/pet-life-cycle-assessment-report-2023/).

PET resin production generates roughly 2.2-2.7 kg CO2e per kg of resin; a single 500ml bottle's raw-material plus production footprint is on the order of 0.04-0.09 kg CO2e. Across the full lifecycle (raw material through end-of-life), published meta-analyses put PET packaging around 5 kg CO2e per kg of material processed. Unlike electronics or textiles, Raw Materials/Manufacturing for plastic packaging is comparatively low-impact per unit; End of Life (landfill vs. recycling vs. incineration) is the more environmentally consequential and variable stage - recycled PET can cut lifecycle emissions by roughly 12-56% depending on the study and recycling pathway.

## General guidance for categories without a dedicated section above

Apply the same proportional-weighting principle instead of distributing impact evenly across all five stages - identify whether a category's footprint is materials-dominated, energy-dominated, or disposal-dominated, and weight the stages accordingly:

- Wood/paper furniture and home goods: Raw Materials (sourcing/deforestation) and Transportation (bulk, low-density freight) tend to dominate.
- Food/groceries: Raw Materials (farming, land and water use) dominate, and for perishables Transportation (cold chain) is significant; Manufacturing and End of Life are typically minor by comparison.
- Durable appliances and electronics-adjacent goods: follow the electronics pattern above - Manufacturing dominates.

For any category without a matched section, state plainly that the estimate is a rough analogy to a related category, not a study-backed figure for that category itself.
