# How to Recycle Common Materials

## Paper/Cardboard
Paper comprises 23% of annual municipal waste. Americans recycled about 68% of paper used in 2018. Most community programs accept newspapers, magazines, and cardboard boxes.

- Pizza boxes: Yes, pizza boxes can be recycled even if they have grease in them — remove food scraps and flatten first.
- Mail: Recyclable, including envelopes with plastic windows.
- Takeout containers: Must be scraped clean; "spatula-clean" is acceptable.
- Shredded paper: Depends on local program.
- Books: Donate if usable; paperbacks recycle as-is, remove hardcover covers first.
- Gift wrap: Laminated wrap typically isn't recyclable; reuse bags instead.

## Plastics
Only 8.7% of the 35+ million tons generated in 2018 was recycled. Rules vary significantly by locality.

- Plastic bags/films: Cannot go in household bins; take to retail stores.
- Bottles and caps: Generally acceptable; caps can remain on bottles.
- Styrofoam: Very few localities accept styrofoam in curbside recycling.
- Containers with food: Rinse thoroughly; "spatula-clean" is sufficient.
- Compostable plastics: Should not enter recycling streams; find composting drop-offs instead.
- Resin codes: Identify plastic type but don't guarantee local acceptance.

## Glass
12.3 million tons generated in 2018; 31.3% recycled. Can be recycled infinitely.

- Mixed colors/types: Most programs accept together and sort at facilities.
- Broken glass: Never recycle; harms workers and equipment.
- Metal caps: Remove and recycle separately.

## Aluminum
3.9 million tons generated in 2018; 34.9% recycling rate.

- Cans: Do not crush (complicates sorting in single-stream facilities).
- Foil: Recyclable if food residue is removed.

## Batteries
Most require special handling — do not place in household or municipal recycling bins.

- Dry-cell: Look for in-store bins or community collection events.
- Lithium-ion: Do NOT put them in the trash or municipal recycling bins.
- Lead-acid: Return to retailers or hazardous waste programs.
- Other rechargeable types: Require specialty disposal.

## Electronics
2.7 million tons generated in 2018; 38.5% recycled. Cannot use curbside pickup. Drop off at collection sites. Delete personal information beforehand. Manufacturers and retailers provide donation/recycling options.

## Food & Yard Materials
Food cannot be recycled. 63.1 million tons of food waste generated in 2018; only 4.1% composted. Composting is recommended. Yard trimmings (35.4 million tons in 2018) cannot be recycled but can be composted or collected through community services.

## Used Oil
Never drain down pipes. One quart of used oil contaminates one million gallons of water. Garages and auto-supply stores typically accept it. One gallon of used oil produces 2.5 quarts of new oil versus 42 gallons of crude oil.

## Household Hazardous Waste
Includes pesticides, paints, solvents, oil filters, light bulbs, batteries, aerosols, antifreeze, and nail polish. Requires special disposal — never pour down drains or discard with regular trash. Use community collection events or permanent centers.

- Paint: Contact local donation centers or hazardous waste facilities.
- Light bulbs: Check local recycling facilities or retail stores.
- Thermometers/barometers: Some are hazardous; consult local facilities.

## Tires & Metal
Garages typically accept used tires when replacing; also available through retailers and collection events. Ferrous metals had a 27.8% recycling rate in 2018; nonferrous metals 68%.

## Miscellaneous
- Diapers: Flush excrement; discard diaper in trash.
- Clothing/shoes: Donate gently worn items to charities; some retailers accept damaged items.
